# High-retention spoken-hook framework v1.0

Use this reference when drafting a new factual-video opening, offering hook options or repairing an opening that is slow, generic, misleading or disconnected from the body.

This is a **spoken-writing** framework. It does not decide shots, B-roll, graphics, camera movement, source acquisition or video generation. A hook must work as narration before a director decides how to show it.

## Four required functions

A strong factual hook normally performs four functions in a compact progression:

    verified spoken anchor → audience entry → compressed question → honest content promise

### 1. Verified spoken anchor

Open with a concrete claim the approved fact card can support: a result, consequence, cost, contrast, decision, action or recognizable problem.

- Prefer one strong fact over a stack of numbers.
- Put required attribution close enough that the opening does not temporarily mislead.
- The first sentence may interpret a fact forcefully, but the interpretation cannot exceed its evidence or causality status.
- Do not depend on an unseen shot to make an empty sentence interesting.

### 2. Audience entry

Show why the fact matters to the intended viewer through a real decision, risk, cost, contradiction or familiar work problem.

- Audience involvement does not require saying “你”.
- Never invent a common experience, consequence or fear merely to manufacture relevance.
- Move from the case to the viewer only as far as the fact card and editorial thesis justify.

### 3. Compressed question

Reduce the topic to one knowledge gap the video can actually close.

- Ask one governing question rather than a bundle of “what, why and how”.
- Do not use a “why X caused Y” question when the evidence establishes only sequence, coexistence or design choices.
- Prefer formulations such as what was designed differently, what condition was missing, what the case can establish or where the boundary sits when causality is not verified.

### 4. Honest content promise

Tell the viewer what will become clearer by the end.

- Promise an explanation, distinction, mechanism, comparison or decision framework that the body actually delivers.
- Promise a test, investigation, interview, action or result only when the approved material contains that work.
- Avoid generic phrases such as “这条视频聊清楚” unless the object of “聊清楚” is precise.
- Do not promise visuals; visual execution belongs to the director.

## Three-route comparison

Before writing the full narration, draft three hook routes that differ in **reasoning entry**, not merely wording. Choose only routes the fact card can support.

Useful route families include:

- **Consequence first:** lead with the most decision-relevant verified outcome, then ask what exposed it.
- **Cost or scale contrast:** make a verified difference in money, time, adoption, people or effort immediately legible.
- **Same-task contrast:** compare two verified ways of doing the same job and let the gap create the question.
- **Recognizable pain:** begin with a specific problem the target viewer genuinely encounters, then connect it to the case.
- **Counterintuitive result:** place an expectation beside a verified result that appears to contradict it.
- **Boundary reversal:** correct the likely wrong diagnosis—“the failure was not the whole transformation; it was this stage or design”—without repeating a disclaimer later.
- **Case-to-rule:** start with one bounded case, then ask what transferable condition it reveals.

### Interest-cliff route

When the audience is likely to think the topic is unrelated to them, an interest-cliff hook may compress the mechanism into a direct consequence:

    if the audience takes a recognizable action → a relevant consequence may follow → the case shows a safer or more useful alternative

Use a conditional consequence only when it is source-supported or logically entailed by the stated setup. Do not invent an extreme loss, medical harm, legal penalty, missed revenue, job loss or failure rate merely to intensify the first sentence. The consequence must remain true without relying on a later disclaimer.

After the consequence, use a short landing line, then introduce the case or distinction that resolves it. Avoid repeating the same warning in the next paragraph.

Do not force a route family that requires missing evidence. Three routes must not be three phrasings of the same number or conclusion.

Compare candidates on:

1. factual defensibility;
2. specificity of the first sentence;
3. relevance to the target viewer;
4. sharpness of the single knowledge gap;
5. honesty and usefulness of the promise;
6. natural connection to the first body paragraph.

If the user asks for options or wants to select the hook, present the candidates as an intermediate deliverable with, for each route:

- opening lines;
- reasoning entry;
- strength;
- factual or structural risk.

Do not add visual directions. If the user asks directly for a complete script, compare the routes internally, select the strongest and proceed without adding a fifth section to the final package.

## Opening timing and density

- Aim for a real viewing reason in the first spoken sentence and roughly the first five seconds.
- Establish the governing question or promise shortly afterward; exact seconds depend on sentence density and delivery.
- Use only the minimum identity, date, source and context needed to understand the anchor.
- Do not restate the opening fact in successive paragraphs. Each following sentence must add attribution, scale, audience stake, question or promise.
- Do not reveal the final transferable judgment so completely that the body has no remaining knowledge gap.

## Failure patterns

Reject or rewrite an opening that:

- starts with chronology or background before stakes;
- relies on unsupported catastrophe, superlative or causal language;
- uses an impressive but irrelevant number;
- says “你可能也……” without a defensible audience relationship;
- asks several broad questions;
- delays attribution until after a source-specific claim has already been treated as objective fact;
- promises an investigation, test or demonstration that does not exist;
- uses “今天聊聊”“最近有件事”“你知道吗” as the main hook;
- requires a shot description to become meaningful;
- repeats the same premise after the viewer already understands it.

## Final hook check

Before locking the opening, answer yes to all applicable questions:

1. Does the first sentence contain a specific, approved factual or interpretive anchor?
2. Is sensitive attribution close enough to prevent a false first impression?
3. Does the target viewer have a real reason to care?
4. Is there one governing knowledge gap?
5. Does the promise state what the body will genuinely resolve?
6. Does the next paragraph advance the story instead of repeating the hook?
7. Can the narration stand without any assumed visual treatment?
