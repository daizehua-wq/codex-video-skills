# HKRR final-script review

Run HKRR after the first draft, factual pass, compression and read-aloud pass. HKRR evaluates viewing quality; it does not choose the story's core thesis or replace factual validation.

## H — Happiness

Happiness means viewing ease and interest, not forced comedy.

Check whether the script has:

- contrast, freshness or a useful surprise;
- concrete situations or facts the viewer can picture;
- an “原来如此” moment;
- manageable cognitive load;
- limited abstract lecturing.

Threshold: `3/5`.

If it fails, prefer a stronger verified contrast, concrete manifestation or vivid factual detail. Do not add jokes merely to raise H.

## K — Knowledge

Check whether the viewer gains:

- a genuinely new fact, mechanism or distinction;
- a reusable judgment framework;
- evidence that actually supports the conclusion;
- a deeper understanding than the opening premise alone supplied.

Threshold: `4/5`.

If it fails, strengthen the mechanism, evidence or decision framework. Do not pile on unrelated numbers.

## R — Resonance

Check whether:

- the core problem comes from the target viewer's real work;
- the viewer can map the issue to their own company or role;
- the script names a problem they feel but may not have articulated;
- the conflict matters to people judging, implementing or governing enterprise AI.

Threshold: `4/5`.

If it fails, revisit the audience, opening problem and real decision context. Do not manufacture emotion.

## Rhythm

Check whether:

- viewing motivation appears quickly;
- long stretches never pass without new information;
- each case performs one necessary job;
- transitions arise from the previous answer's next question;
- the script keeps producing cognitive progress;
- it stops once the conclusion is earned.

Threshold: `4/5`.

If it fails, remove repetition, compress cases, advance the conflict or reorder questions. Do not delete necessary logic simply to shorten the script.

## Revision rule

Final thresholds:

| Dimension | Minimum |
|---|---:|
| H | 3/5 |
| K | 4/5 |
| R | 4/5 |
| Rhythm | 4/5 |

If any dimension is below threshold:

1. identify the lowest-scoring dimension or tied dimensions;
2. revise only the problem that caused that score first;
3. preserve all fact-card wording, attribution and scope limits;
4. rescore the complete revised script;
5. do not report completion while a final score remains below threshold.

The human-facing review must include the four scores, a specific rationale for each, the weakest dimension, whether thresholds are met and whether another revision is recommended.
