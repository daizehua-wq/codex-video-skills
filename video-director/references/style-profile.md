# Default Style Profile: Evidence-led Talking Head v1.0

Use this profile when the project has adopted the reference style and has not supplied a more specific override.

## Coverage and rhythm

- Target B-roll coverage: 60–70% of finished editorial content.
- Typical B-roll block: 8–40 seconds.
- Typical shot inside a block: 2–10 seconds.
- Review uninterrupted full-screen A-roll longer than 20 seconds. Keep it only when performance, emotion or a deliberate pause is carrying the scene.
- Use 3–15 second full-screen A-roll resets between major evidence blocks when the narration structure permits.
- Coverage is a project-level measure. Do not lengthen an irrelevant visual or repeat a shot merely to satisfy the percentage.

## Layout grammar

Default B-roll layout:

- B-roll occupies the dominant 55–70% of the composition.
- The speaker remains visible in an 18–28% picture-in-picture when verbal continuity or trust benefits from a visible narrator.
- Full-screen B-roll is appropriate when a document, interface or quoted evidence must be read.
- Full-screen A-roll is appropriate for thesis statements, reversals, transitions, emotional emphasis and resets.
- Keep narration subtitles separate from source text. Do not stack multiple subtitle systems over the same region.

These percentages guide composition rather than mandate exact pixels. Respect safe areas and the project aspect ratio.

## Visual roles

Assign exactly one primary role to each request:

- `proof`: directly supports a factual claim.
- `demonstration`: shows a real product, operation or observable behavior.
- `explanation`: makes a mechanism, sequence or relationship understandable.
- `context`: establishes a real person, place, company or period.
- `reset`: a brief non-evidentiary visual transition explicitly requested by the director.

For `proof`, require a source-identifiable frame. Generic stock, unattributed data cards and AI-generated images cannot prove facts.

For `explanation`, distinguish a source-backed diagram from an illustrative reconstruction. Never present a reconstruction as an official internal architecture.

## Source preference

Use this default order unless the request provides a narrower order:

1. Official corporate or product source.
2. Legal, regulatory or other primary document.
3. Authoritative media.
4. Named partner or technology supplier.
5. Licensed generic stock for explicitly generic scenes.

Source priority does not override project consistency. A lower-tier source that depicts the correct product is preferable to a prestigious but unrelated image.

## Mobile readability

For pages, documents, slides and interfaces:

- identify the exact title, metric, paragraph, control or workflow region that must be readable;
- request a source-identifiable wide view and a readable detail view when both identity and text matter;
- avoid full-page screenshots whose important text becomes illegible at delivery size;
- keep enough surrounding context to prevent a crop from changing the meaning;
- reserve full-screen treatment for evidence that genuinely needs reading.

## Avoid

- random office, keyboard, city or server footage used only to cover a cut;
- futuristic robots or abstract AI animations for a specific real product;
- a generic chatbot standing in for the named customer-service system;
- a related plugin, marketing surface or old interview standing in for the requested product or period;
- one static screenshot held for an entire long argument block;
- tiny source material surrounded by unused decorative space;
- repeated source footage with no new informational purpose.
