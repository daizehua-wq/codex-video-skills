# Default Style Profile: Comprehension-led Talking Head v3.0

Use this profile when the project has not supplied a stronger visual language.

## Governing principle

A visual must make a specific idea easier to understand, verify or observe. A-roll is not empty space to cover. Do not target a B-roll percentage, minimum shot count or maximum uninterrupted A-roll duration.

The preferred argument rhythm is:

> presenter frames the question → visual proves, explains or demonstrates → presenter interprets or concludes

Use this rhythm at meaningful argument boundaries, not mechanically for every sentence.

After planning, report the observed duration of each visual mode. Do not grade the plan by coverage.

## Opening

- Begin with the locked script's conflict, abnormal result or strongest question when available.
- Do not insert a logo bumper, generic montage or scene-setting footage before the conflict.
- A short preview montage is useful only when every image previews a later entity, source, mechanism or consequence.
- If a reusable visual anchor is central to the explanation, preview its purpose early without forcing the audience to read the entire diagram.

## Reusable visual anchors

Use a relationship map, timeline, process, comparison board or layered system when several beats revisit the same entities or state.

An anchor should:

- place recurring entities consistently;
- keep colors and labels stable;
- reveal one layer, relation, amount or state at a time;
- preserve previously learned context;
- dim or crop unrelated regions instead of rebuilding a new diagram;
- remain readable on the target aspect ratio.

Do not use a master canvas when the topic has no recurring system or when a simple source or A-roll explanation is clearer.

## Mode selection

### A-roll

Keep full-screen A-roll for:

- arguments already clear in spoken language;
- interpretation, judgment and opinion;
- chapter questions, transitions and scope caveats;
- emotional emphasis and final takeaways;
- any beat where a proposed visual adds no new information.

Long A-roll is acceptable. Use framing changes or brief text emphasis only when they serve the meaning.

### Source evidence

Use for news, reports, speeches, documents, quotations and factual disclosures.

Default evidence sequence:

1. establish the original source through publisher, title, date, speaker or document identity;
2. move to the exact paragraph, quotation, number, timestamp or table cell;
3. highlight only the words relevant to the narration;
4. optionally add a translation that remains visually subordinate to the original;
5. state editorial interpretation separately;
6. leave after normal-speed viewers have had time to read the evidence.

Use the original source capture. Do not replace it with a re-typeset evidence card. A derived annotation layer may crop, mask or highlight the original without rewriting it.

### Product demonstration

Use a real interface, screen recording or official demonstration when narration depends on what the product does or how a workflow behaves. Show the named product surface, action and result. A logo or marketing page alone is not a demonstration.

### Motion graphics

Use for mechanisms, hierarchies, taxonomies, sequences, dependencies, state changes and comparisons that are cumbersome to understand by listening alone.

Every animation beat must define:

- the visual state before the beat;
- the action or transition;
- the visual state after the beat;
- the new information now available to the viewer.

If the information gain is empty, remove the motion. Static elements may remain static.

### Illustrative metaphor

Use only when a non-literal analogy materially reduces the burden of an abstract relationship, incentive or risk. The metaphor may interpret verified facts; it cannot establish them.

- Keep the non-literal framing obvious.
- Record how a viewer could misread it.
- Disclose AI generation visibly when applicable.
- Do not reconstruct an alleged real event or place generated characters inside source-evidence treatment.
- Return to A-roll or evidence for the final factual judgment.

### Text emphasis

Use brief text over A-roll for a number, keyword, contrast or conclusion that benefits from emphasis but does not require proof or explanation. It should disappear after the emphasis is complete.

## Presenter continuity

Choose presenter visibility by function:

- `full_frame`: question, interpretation, judgment, reset or conclusion;
- `split_screen`: presenter and comparison must be read together;
- `pip`: a longer source, map or demonstration benefits from a visible guide;
- `absent`: full-screen reading or observation is more important.

Picture-in-picture is optional. Do not cover source identity, evidence, interface controls or subtitles. For long visual explanations, stable presenter PIP can preserve human continuity without interrupting the explanation.

## Layout and readability

- Use full-screen source evidence when the audience must read it.
- On vertical video, divide large diagrams into stable regions rather than shrinking the complete canvas.
- Keep narration subtitles, presenter and evidence on separate visual layers.
- A source-wide view and detail view are treatment beats using the same acquired asset, not automatically separate acquisition requests.
- Bind numbers to the relevant entity, proportion, place or direction; do not float large numbers without context.

## Still-image motion

Do not apply a generic push-in to every still.

Movement is justified only when it changes what the audience can identify or read, such as:

- wide source page to headline;
- headline to date or named speaker;
- paragraph to exact quotation;
- table to relevant cell;
- complete map to a named layer;
- full scene to a number anchored to a real location;
- before view to after view.

If motion reveals no new information, hold the frame or keep A-roll.

## Ending

- Resolve or qualify the opening question before the outro.
- Use A-roll for the final factual judgment, uncertainty or scope caveat.
- Do not introduce new evidence after the conclusion.
- Outro visuals may recap established entities, anchor states or metaphors but must not create new claims.
- Do not extend an outro only to create recommendation-screen inventory; duration follows the delivery platform and user requirement.

## Avoid

- coverage targets used to generate shot needs;
- decorative B-roll, generic office footage or unrelated AI imagery;
- a sequence of similarly styled cards that merely repeats narration;
- re-typeset quotations presented as original evidence;
- continuous B-roll blocks assembled only to avoid showing the presenter;
- picture-in-picture covering readable source material;
- motion described only as cinematic, dynamic, engaging or rhythmic;
- AI-generated scenes presented as real events;
- metaphors used as factual proof;
- multiple visual systems competing with narration subtitles.
